Homework 4 · Sampling and Sampling Distributions

CRJU 705 · due before noon Tuesday ahead of Session 5

Instructions: complete the following exercises in a single .R script file. Include comments explaining your code and answers to any interpretive questions. Submit your .R file via Canvas for credit.

Start your script with:

library(tidyverse)
set.seed(705)   # so your "random" results are reproducible when I run your script

scores <- read_csv("https://smourtgos.github.io/crju705/data/population-data.csv")

Part 1: Understanding the Population

  1. Make a histogram of the score column. Describe the general shape of the distribution in a comment.

  2. Calculate the mean, median, and standard deviation of score.

Part 2: Sampling from the Population

  1. Draw a random sample of size 30 from score, without replacement (that’s sample()’s default). Calculate the mean and standard deviation of the sample.

  2. Repeat Question 3 five times, recording the sample mean each time. (Copy-paste is fine; replicate(5, mean(sample(scores$score, size = 30))) is nicer.) How much do the five means vary?

  3. Interpretation (in a comment): compare your sample means to the population mean. Why are they close but not identical?

Part 3: Sampling from Unknown Distributions

  1. Imagine you did not know the population distribution. Draw one new sample of size 30, make a histogram of it, and describe in a comment what type of distribution the sample appears to come from.

  2. Interpretation (in a comment): could your sample lead you to believe the population has a different shape than it really does? Explain.

Part 4: Build a Sampling Distribution

  1. Draw 500 samples of size 30 and keep each sample’s mean. Two equivalent options — use either:

    many_means <- replicate(500, mean(sample(scores$score, size = 30)))

# or, tidyverse-style, as in the Session 4 slides:
many_means <- tibble(draw = 1:500) |>
  mutate(mean_score = map_dbl(draw, \(d) mean(sample(scores$score, size = 30))))

  2. Make a histogram of the 500 sample means. In a comment: how does its shape and spread compare to the histogram of raw scores from Part 1?

  3. Compute the standard deviation of your 500 sample means, and compare it to the formula for the standard error, sd(scores$score) / sqrt(30). How close are they? What is this quantity called?

Part 5: Real Data — The CLT with Chicago Arrests

  1. Load the course anchor data and compute the overall arrest proportion — treat these 30,000 incidents as your population:

    crimes <- read_csv("https://smourtgos.github.io/crju705/data/chicago-crimes-2025.csv")
mean(crimes$arrest)

  2. Simulate an analyst who can only pull 100 case files at a time: draw 500 samples of 100 incidents each, computing the proportion arrested in each sample. Make a histogram of the 500 proportions.

  3. Interpretation (in a comment): each individual incident’s arrest value is just TRUE or FALSE — about as non-normal as data can be. What shape does your histogram of sample proportions have, and what theorem from Session 4 explains that?

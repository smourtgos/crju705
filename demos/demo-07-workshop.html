Four real criminal justice questions, one recipe: count, test, say it

What this page is. The Session 7 lecture, written down step by step. Every chunk below is self-contained: copy any of them into your own R session and it runs as-is. Use this page to catch up after a missed class, or to re-run the examples at your own pace.

What you’ll build

Four answers to four real questions, each with the same recipe:

  1. Question. Name the two groups and the outcome. A yes/no outcome means prop.test() and bayes_prop_test(); a number means t.test().
  2. Count or describe.
  3. Test.
  4. Say it. The honest sentence, how big in real units, and one thing the test cannot tell you.

The questions: did financial aid at release lower re-arrest? Were employed arrestees released more often? How much lower is income in Chicago’s high-violence areas? Was body camera footage reported more often in fatal police shootings after 2020?

Each example also has the animation from class. Press Play under each one, or drag the slider to step through it at your own pace. The code that draws them is folded away (click “Code” to see it). You do not need to write code like that; it is there so nothing is hidden.

1 · Reentry: does money at release keep people out of jail?

432 men released from Maryland state prisons in the 1970s were followed for one year. Half were randomly assigned to receive financial aid (fin). The outcome is arrest: 1 if arrested again within the year. One row is one man.

rossi |> count(fin, arrest)
# A tibble: 4 × 3
  fin   arrest     n
  <chr>  <dbl> <int>
1 no         0   150
2 no         1    66
3 yes        0   168
4 yes        1    48

Aid: 48 re-arrested out of 48 + 168 = 216 (22.2%). No aid: 66 out of 66 + 150 = 216 (30.6%). Aid goes first, so every difference reads Aid minus No aid.

prop.test(x = c(48, 66), n = c(216, 216))

    2-sample test for equality of proportions with continuity correction

data:  c(48, 66) out of c(216, 216)
X-squared = 3.4439, df = 1, p-value = 0.06349
alternative hypothesis: two.sided
95 percent confidence interval:
 -0.170713151  0.004046484
sample estimates:
   prop 1    prop 2 
0.2222222 0.3055556

The interval runs from about 17 points lower to 0.4 points higher, and p = 0.063. Zero is just inside, so: fail to reject the null. A funder who reads only that line cuts the program.

What is 0.063 measuring? Watch it. Pretend aid did nothing: everyone has the same 26% chance of re-arrest. Run the study again and again, and pile up the gaps that luck alone produces:

Code
set.seed(705)
pooled <- (48 + 66) / 432
null_gaps <- tibble(gap = 100 * (rbinom(10000, 216, pooled) - rbinom(10000, 216, pooled)) / 216)
step_size <- 100 / 216
steps <- c(20, 100, 500, 2000, 10000)
rossi_anim <- map_dfr(steps, \(k) {
  first_k <- slice_head(null_gaps, n = k)
  first_k |>
    mutate(bin = round(gap / step_size)) |>
    count(bin) |>
    complete(bin = -45:45, fill = list(n = 0)) |>
    mutate(share = 100 * n / k, bin = bin * step_size,
           as_big = if_else(abs(bin) >= 8.3, "At least as big as ours", "Smaller than ours"),
           label = paste(format(k, big.mark = ","), "pretend studies"),
           pct_big = 100 * mean(abs(first_k$gap) >= 8.3))
}) |>
  mutate(label = factor(label, levels = unique(label)))
rossi_text <- rossi_anim |>
  distinct(label, pct_big) |>
  mutate(x = 13.5, y = 7.2,
         txt = paste0(sprintf("%.1f", pct_big), "% had a gap<br>at least as big as ours"))

plot_ly(height = 470) |>
  add_bars(data = rossi_anim, x = ~bin, y = ~share, frame = ~label, color = ~as_big,
           colors = c("firebrick", "gray75"), hoverinfo = "none") |>
  add_text(data = rossi_text, x = ~x, y = ~y, text = ~txt, frame = ~label,
           textfont = list(size = 16, color = "firebrick"), showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  layout(barmode = "overlay",
         xaxis = list(title = "Gap in re-arrest rate, aid minus no aid (points)", range = c(-20.5, 20.5)),
         yaxis = list(title = "Share of pretend studies (%)", range = c(0, 8)),
         shapes = list(
           list(type = "line", x0 = -8.3, x1 = -8.3, y0 = 0, y1 = 8, line = list(color = "navy", width = 3)),
           list(type = "line", x0 = 8.3, x1 = 8.3, y0 = 0, y1 = 8, line = list(color = "navy", width = 3, dash = "dash"))),
         annotations = list(list(x = -8.6, y = 7.8, text = "Our study: -8.3", showarrow = FALSE,
                                 xanchor = "right", font = list(size = 14, color = "navy"))),
         legend = list(orientation = "h", y = 1.08)) |>
  animation_opts(frame = 1500, transition = 700, redraw = FALSE) |>
  animation_slider(currentvalue = list(prefix = "")) |>
  config(displayModeBar = FALSE)

In a world where aid did nothing, about 5 studies in 100 still show a gap of 8.3 points or more, in one direction or the other. That is what p = 0.063 is measuring (prop.test() adds a small correction, so its number is a little bigger). Not rare enough to pass the 0.05 bar. Now ask the other question:

bayes_prop_test(x = c(48, 66), n = c(216, 216))

    Bayesian test of two proportions 

data, group 1:  48 of 216 (22.2%)
data, group 2:  66 of 216 (30.6%)
prior:          know-nothing (1 yes, 1 no), same for both groups

                         estimate      95% credible interval
group 1                  22.5%         17.2% to 28.2%
group 2                  30.7%         24.8% to 37.0%
difference (1 minus 2)   -8.2 points   -16.5 to 0.0 points

Probability group 1 is higher than group 2: 0.03 
Probability group 1 is lower than group 2:  0.97

Say it. Given these data, there is about a 97% probability that men who got aid were re-arrested at a lower rate. About 22 of every 100 men with aid were re-arrested, against about 31 of every 100 without: roughly 8 fewer re-arrests per 100 people released. What this cannot tell you: whether the effect lasts past a year, or would hold today. “Not significant” is not “no effect.”

2 · Pretrial release: does a job get you released?

5,226 Toronto arrests for possessing small amounts of marijuana, 1997 to 2002. released is whether the person was released with a summons instead of held for a bail hearing. The group is employed. One row is one arrest.

arrests |> count(employed, released)
# A tibble: 4 × 3
  employed released     n
  <chr>    <chr>    <int>
1 No       No         349
2 No       Yes        766
3 Yes      No         543
4 Yes      Yes       3568

Employed: 3,568 released out of 3,568 + 543 = 4,111 (86.8%). Not employed: 766 out of 766 + 349 = 1,115 (68.7%).

prop.test(x = c(3568, 766), n = c(4111, 1115))

data, group 1:  3,568 of 4,111 (86.8%)
data, group 2:  766 of 1,115 (68.7%)

                         estimate      95% credible interval

About 15 to 21 points more often for the employed, by either test, and more than a 99% probability the employed rate is higher. But the two groups differ in other ways. checks counts how many of six police databases (prior arrests, convictions, parole, and so on) had the person’s name:

arrests |> summarize(avg_checks = mean(checks), people = n(), .by = employed)
# A tibble: 2 × 3
  employed avg_checks people
  <chr>         <dbl>  <int>
1 Yes            1.44   4111
2 No             2.35   1115

People without a job had more of a record, and a record is a reason to hold someone. Watch the gap shrink as we keep only people with less and less of a record:

Code
cutoffs <- tibble(k = c(6, 3, 2, 1, 0),
                  label = c("Everyone", "In 3 or fewer databases", "In 2 or fewer",
                            "In 1 or fewer", "No record at all"))
tor_anim <- cutoffs |>
  mutate(d = map(k, \(kk) arrests |> filter(checks <= kk) |>
                   summarize(rate = 100 * mean(released == "Yes"), people = n(), .by = employed))) |>
  unnest(d) |>
  mutate(employed = if_else(employed == "Yes", "Employed", "Not employed"),
         xpos = if_else(employed == "Employed", 1, 2),
         label = factor(label, levels = cutoffs$label),
         txt = paste0(round(rate, 1), "%<br>(", format(people, big.mark = ",", trim = TRUE), " people)"))
tor_gap <- tor_anim |>
  summarize(gap = rate[employed == "Employed"] - rate[employed == "Not employed"], .by = label) |>
  mutate(x = 1.5, y = 104, txt = paste0("Gap: ", round(gap, 1), " points"))

plot_ly(height = 470) |>
  add_bars(data = tor_anim, x = ~xpos, y = ~rate, frame = ~label, color = ~employed,
           colors = c("firebrick", "steelblue"), text = ~txt, textposition = "inside",
           insidetextfont = list(size = 15, color = "white"), hoverinfo = "none", showlegend = FALSE) |>
  add_text(data = tor_gap, x = ~x, y = ~y, text = ~txt, frame = ~label,
           textfont = list(size = 18, color = "navy"), showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  layout(xaxis = list(title = "", tickvals = c(1, 2), ticktext = c("Employed", "Not employed"),
                      range = c(0.4, 2.6)),
         yaxis = list(title = "Released with a summons (%)", range = c(0, 110))) |>
  animation_opts(frame = 1600, transition = 800, redraw = FALSE) |>
  animation_slider(currentvalue = list(prefix = "Who is included: ")) |>
  config(displayModeBar = FALSE)

Say it. Among people with clean records, the employed were released about 10 points more often (92.5% vs 82.6%; interval about 4 to 16 points, credible interval 5.1 to 15.8 points, more than a 99% probability the employed rate is higher). The gap shrank from 18 points to 10 when we compared like with like, but it did not disappear. What this cannot tell you: whether some other difference (age, citizenship, where the arrest happened) explains the rest.

3 · Neighborhoods: violence and income

Chicago’s 77 community areas. high_violence marks the 38 areas whose violent-crime rate is above the city’s median. The outcome is median_income, a number in dollars, so the test is t.test(). One row is one area, not one person. First, name the groups and put High violence first:

areas <- areas |>
  mutate(violence = factor(if_else(high_violence, "High violence", "Other areas"),
                           levels = c("High violence", "Other areas")))

areas |>
  summarize(avg_income = mean(median_income), areas = n(), .by = violence) |>
  arrange(violence)
# A tibble: 2 × 3
  violence      avg_income areas
  <fct>              <dbl> <int>
1 High violence     54355.    38
2 Other areas       90088.    39

See it. Each dot is one community area:

Code
set.seed(705)
jit <- runif(nrow(areas), -0.18, 0.18)
stage_labels <- c("All 77 areas", "Split by violence", "The two averages")
chi_pts <- map_dfr(1:3, \(s) areas |>
  mutate(x = if (s == 1) 1.5 + jit else if_else(violence == "High violence", 1, 2) + jit,
         stage = stage_labels[s])) |>
  mutate(stage = factor(stage, levels = stage_labels),
         hover = paste0(community, "<br>$", format(round(median_income), big.mark = ",")))
chi_means <- areas |>
  summarize(avg = mean(median_income), .by = violence)
chi_means <- map_dfr(1:3, \(s) chi_means |>
  mutate(x = if (s < 3) -1 else if_else(violence == "High violence", 1, 2),
         stage = stage_labels[s])) |>
  mutate(stage = factor(stage, levels = stage_labels),
         txt = paste0("Average<br>$", format(round(avg), big.mark = ",")))
chi_gap <- tibble(stage = factor(stage_labels, levels = stage_labels),
                  x = c(-1, -1, 1.5), y = 175000, txt = "Gap: about $35,700")

plot_ly(height = 470) |>
  add_markers(data = chi_pts, x = ~x, y = ~median_income, frame = ~stage, ids = ~community,
              color = ~violence, colors = c("firebrick", "steelblue"),
              marker = list(size = 10, opacity = 0.8), text = ~hover, hoverinfo = "text") |>
  add_markers(data = chi_means, x = ~x, y = ~avg, frame = ~stage, ids = ~violence,
              marker = list(symbol = "line-ew-open", size = 60, color = "navy",
                            line = list(width = 4, color = "navy")),
              showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  add_text(data = chi_means, x = ~x + 0.33, y = ~avg, text = ~txt, frame = ~stage,
           textfont = list(size = 14, color = "navy"), showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  add_text(data = chi_gap, x = ~x, y = ~y, text = ~txt, frame = ~stage,
           textfont = list(size = 18, color = "navy"), showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  layout(xaxis = list(title = "", range = c(0.4, 2.6), tickvals = c(1, 2),
                      ticktext = c("High violence", "Other areas")),
         yaxis = list(title = "Median household income", tickprefix = "$", range = c(0, 190000)),
         legend = list(orientation = "h", y = 1.08)) |>

t.test(median_income ~ violence, data = areas)

    Welch Two Sample t-test

data:  median_income by violence
t = -6.3561, df = 74.959, p-value = 1.458e-08
alternative hypothesis: true difference in means between group High violence and group Other areas is not equal to 0
95 percent confidence interval:
 -46932.44 -24533.58
sample estimates:
mean in group High violence   mean in group Other areas 
                   54354.63                    90087.64

Say it. High-violence areas minus other areas: about $25,000 to $47,000 lower median household income ($54,355 vs $90,088 on average), p = 0.00000001. The unit is dollars because the column is dollars. What this cannot tell you: which way it runs (does poverty breed violence, or does violence drive income out?), and anything about individual people. Each row is an area.

4 · Use of force: body cameras over time

The Washington Post’s database of every person shot and killed by on-duty police in the U.S., 2015 to 2024. body_camera is TRUE when news or police reports said a camera recorded the incident. One row is one person killed. Group: before and after 2020.

shootings <- shootings |>
  mutate(period = if_else(year(date) >= 2020, "2020 to 2024", "2015 to 2019"))

shootings |> count(period, body_camera)
# A tibble: 4 × 3
  period       body_camera     n
  <chr>        <lgl>       <int>
1 2015 to 2019 FALSE        4329
2 2015 to 2019 TRUE          594
3 2020 to 2024 FALSE        4302
4 2020 to 2024 TRUE         1205

2020 to 2024: 1,205 of 1,205 + 4,302 = 5,507 (21.9%). 2015 to 2019: 594 of 594 + 4,329 = 4,923 (12.1%).

Year by year first, because two five-year averages can hide a lot:

Code
yearly <- shootings |>
  mutate(year = year(date)) |>
  summarize(pct = 100 * mean(body_camera), .by = c(year, period)) |>
  arrange(year)
yr_labels <- c(as.character(2015:2024), "The two periods")
bwc_anim <- map_dfr(seq_along(yr_labels), \(s) yearly |>
  mutate(shown = if_else(s <= 10 & year > 2014 + s, 0, pct), step = yr_labels[s])) |>
  mutate(step = factor(step, levels = yr_labels),
         txt = if_else(shown > 0, paste0(round(shown), "%"), ""))
per <- shootings |> summarize(pct = 100 * mean(body_camera), .by = period)
bwc_lines <- map_dfr(seq_along(yr_labels), \(s) per |>
  mutate(x = if (s < 11) 2010 else if_else(period == "2015 to 2019", 2017, 2022),
         step = yr_labels[s])) |>
  mutate(step = factor(step, levels = yr_labels),
         ytxt = if_else(period == "2015 to 2019", 19.5, 35),
         txt = paste0(period, ": ", round(pct, 1), "%"))

plot_ly(height = 470) |>
  add_bars(data = bwc_anim, x = ~year, y = ~shown, frame = ~step, color = ~period,
           colors = c("steelblue", "firebrick"), text = ~txt, textposition = "outside",
           hoverinfo = "none") |>
  add_markers(data = bwc_lines, x = ~x, y = ~pct, frame = ~step, ids = ~period,
              marker = list(symbol = "line-ew-open", size = 200, color = "navy",
                            line = list(width = 4, color = "navy")),
              showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  add_text(data = bwc_lines, x = ~x, y = ~ytxt, text = ~txt, frame = ~step,
           textfont = list(size = 15, color = "navy"), showlegend = FALSE, hoverinfo = "none") |>
  layout(xaxis = list(title = "", range = c(2014.4, 2024.6), dtick = 1),
         yaxis = list(title = "Body camera reported (%)", range = c(0, 38)),
         legend = list(orientation = "h", y = 1.08)) |>
  animation_opts(frame = 900, transition = 400, redraw = FALSE) |>
  animation_slider(currentvalue = list(prefix = "")) |>
  config(displayModeBar = FALSE)

The rise is real but not smooth: 2023 hit 30%, and 2024 fell back to 20%. Comparing two five-year periods averages over those bumps; that is a choice, and an honest write-up says so. Now both tests on the two periods:

prop.test(x = c(1205, 594), n = c(5507, 4923))

    2-sample test for equality of proportions with continuity correction

data:  c(1205, 594) out of c(5507, 4923)
X-squared = 174.76, df = 1, p-value < 2.2e-16
alternative hypothesis: two.sided
95 percent confidence interval:
 0.08374831 0.11256026
sample estimates:
   prop 1    prop 2 
0.2188124 0.1206581 
bayes_prop_test(x = c(1205, 594), n = c(5507, 4923))

    Bayesian test of two proportions 

data, group 1:  1,205 of 5,507 (21.9%)
data, group 2:  594 of 4,923 (12.1%)
prior:          know-nothing (1 yes, 1 no), same for both groups

                         estimate      95% credible interval
group 1                  21.9%         20.8% to 23.0%
group 2                  12.1%         11.2% to 13.0%
difference (1 minus 2)   9.8 points    8.4 to 11.2 points

Probability group 1 is higher than group 2: more than 0.99 
Probability group 1 is lower than group 2:  less than 0.01

Say it. About 12 in 100 fatal shootings had known camera footage before 2020, and about 22 in 100 after (8 to 11 points higher, by either test). Close to doubling, and still fewer than 1 in 4. What this cannot tell you: whether cameras changed anything about the shootings, or how many cameras existed but went unreported. With 10,430 cases, the p-value was never in doubt. The size is the whole story.

Four questions, one recipe

Test Answer The lesson
Reentry prop.test() + Bayes 22% vs 31%; p = 0.063; 97% probably lower “Not significant” is not “no effect”
Pretrial prop.test() + Bayes, then again after filter() 18 points; 10 among clean records Groups differ in other ways. Compare like with like
Neighborhoods t.test() $25k to $47k lower Say the size in real units. Areas are not people
Use of force prop.test() + Bayes 12% to 22% With a huge n, the p-value adds nothing; the size is the story

Try a variation

  1. Rossi: were men released on parole (paro) re-arrested less often? Count, then both tests, then say it.
  2. Toronto: were citizens (citizen) released more often than non-citizens? Then ask again among people with checks == 0. Does the gap shrink the way the employment gap did?
  3. Chicago areas: run t.test(pct_vacant ~ violence, data = areas). How much higher is the share of vacant housing in high-violence areas, in percentage points?
  4. Shootings: was a body camera reported more often when the person was in a mental health crisis (was_mental_illness_related)? Count, then both tests, and say the size in plain words.
